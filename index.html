Build and maintain a portfolio

  1. Choose one published methodology and an approved data source.
  2. Screen the company’s activity and financial ratios using current filings.
  3. Use halal ETFs or mutual funds when you need ongoing committee oversight.
  4. Calculate purification from the provider’s impure-income ratio and donate it.
  5. Assess zakat separately with a qualified scholar; treatment differs for trading inventory versus long-term holdings.
  6. Re-screen at least quarterly and whenever a material acquisition, debt issue, or business change occurs.

Gray areas and monitoring

Preferred shares, conventional bonds, most derivatives, margin, short selling, and interest-based cash accounts require specific scholarly analysis; do not assume a hedge is permissible merely because it reduces risk.

If a holding fails, stop new purchases, check whether the failure is temporary or data-related, ask the fund/index scholar about the grace period, and exit or purify according to that methodology. Keep dated evidence for every decision.
